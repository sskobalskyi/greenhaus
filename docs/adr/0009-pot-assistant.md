# 9. The pot assistant

Status: accepted

## Context
The spec has an assistant in each pot with tiered access: Novice gets general care answers (10 questions a month, no data), Gardener 100 a month with the current readings, Druid 500 a month with history and the care log. Docs-Brain answers questions over documents with citations and its own daily token budget per tenant, but it knows nothing of tiers, pots or Pulse. It was agreed with the Docs-Brain project that the product rules stay in Greenhaus and Docs-Brain stays generic.

## Decision
- `POST /pots/{potId}/assistant/ask` in the Greenhaus API is the only door. It checks that the pot is the caller's, that the question is 1 to 500 characters and that the monthly quota of the tier is not used up, then asks Docs-Brain and streams the answer to the browser as server-sent events, event by event and unchanged (`sources`, `token`, `done`).
- The quota is a counter per user and calendar month (UTC). A question counts only when the stream ends with a `done` of mode `answer`; a fallback with passages (`retrieval_only`), no result and a broken answer cost nothing. The counter is increased by one statement, so answers that finish together are both counted. Two questions started at the same moment can both pass the check, which can overshoot the quota by the number of simultaneous questions of one person. At the limit the answer is `403 limit_reached` (`ai_questions`, next tier) and `limit.reached.v1` goes out for the funnel. `usage.aiQuestionsThisMonth` in the entitlements is the counter.
- What the assistant is told is written by a pure builder (`AssistantContextBuilder` in `Greenhaus.Domain`) from facts the API gathers, as much as `aiDataAccess` allows: none gives the pot, its plants with what the species prefer and the targets; current adds the latest readings per zone and the open alerts; history adds a min, average and max of the last days (at most 7, and never more than the tier's history) per channel from Pulse, the last done care tasks and the automation log. Pulse being down or lacking the operation leaves a note in the text and never fails the question.
- The text is at most 4000 characters, the limit Docs-Brain accepts. It is plain lines without markdown headings. What a person typed (pot and plant names, notes, titles of their own care tasks) is cleaned, shortened and kept together in one section between `[user-entered text begins]` and `[user-entered text ends]`, introduced as data and not instructions, and a person cannot close the section early because the delimiters are removed from their text. Zone labels are typed by the person too, so zones are named by position. When the text is too long, whole lines of the least important parts are dropped, never half a line or a delimiter.
- Docs-Brain is called with a client-credentials token of the client `greenhaus-assistant` of the shared realm (role `docs-brain.assistant`, audience `docs-brain`), with the person's Keycloak subject as `X-Tenant-Id`: one Docs-Brain tenant per person for now, a household later. Only this service holds the secret, because the role can act for any tenant. The token is cached until shortly before it expires. Nothing is called until a token source is configured (`DocsBrain__TokenUrl` with the client credentials, or a fixed `DocsBrain__StaticToken` for local runs).
- When Docs-Brain cannot be reached, refuses the call (401, 403, 5xx) or breaks off, the person gets `503 assistant_unavailable` if nothing was sent yet, and otherwise the stream is closed with a `done` of mode `retrieval_only` and reason `assistant_unavailable`. It is never a server error and never counted.
- A person may ask 20 questions a minute at most (rate limit per user), on top of Docs-Brain's own limit and daily token budget, which stay as the cost backstop.

## Consequences
- Tier rules live in one place, and Docs-Brain needs no knowledge of Greenhaus beyond a tenant id and a block of facts.
- The assistant only sees what the tier allows, and typed text cannot easily steer it, but it can only be asked to treat that text as data, not forced to.
- The shared care corpus (species care texts visible to all tenants) is not part of this change, so answers rest on the facts and Docs-Brain's own documents.
- The first event is awaited before the answer starts, so a refusal is a proper status code, at the price of a short wait before the browser sees headers.
